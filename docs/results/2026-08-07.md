# 2026-08-07: S2 exit legs, reads from cache and cold attach in tens to hundreds of milliseconds

The S2 exit line on zou#62 makes three claims: cold attach under 300 ms to first row, read tier latencies inside the spec budgets, and the select-only leg that embarrassed v1 on MinIO rerun with its reads served from cache instead of the store.
These are those measurements, taken with the read tier counters PR zou#194 added, through the attach runner zou-bench#8 added.

Two boxes.
gamingpc, i9-13900K, 32 threads, 56 GB, Ubuntu 26.04 under WSL2, idle during the runs, store on local disk.
server3, 8 core EPYC VM, 23 GB, the same MinIO the v1 numbers came from, reached through its docker proxy on 9100.
Both runs are `scripts/run-s2-exit.sh`: build a scale 100 cluster with the page cache and read cache on, checkpoint and let it fold, run select-scale100 through the counters, stop the server, then 20 attach cycles against the same data directory.
server3 was carrying another tenant's load in bursts of 30 to 50 that evening, so its first pass went to the bin and the numbers below are a retake in a quiet window, load 2 when the select leg started and every point of load after that our own 8 clients.
Nothing is simulated in either run.

## select-scale100, where the reads came from

| label | tps | p50 ms | p95 | p99 | reads | tier | tier p50 us | tier p99 us | store gets in window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zou-localfs-gamingpc | 103149 | 0.069 | 0.121 | 0.134 | 10324664 | cache | 8 | 16 | fold only |
| zou-minio-server3 | 3416.7 | 0.883 | 7.032 | 34.137 | 343648 | cache | 64 | 16384 | 12, 8 KB |

Every read both runs made was answered by the cache tier, the pending map and the local page cache.
The local and store tiers took zero calls during either measured window.

The v1 baseline this replaces, same box, same MinIO, same scenario: 37.8 tps at 211.4 ms average, because every read walked to the store, pulling 2.2 GB of checkpoint objects and 1.2 GB of wal during the 60 s window.
The v2 window's entire store read traffic is 12 gets totalling 8 KB, eleven of them the manifest heartbeat.
That is 90x the throughput at 1/240th the median latency, and the difference is exactly the reads that no longer happen.

On gamingpc the op counters do show about 1 GB of store gets during the window, all chk and manifest class.
That is the fold compacting the freshly loaded cluster in the background, and it is the point of the design that it shows up here and not in the read path: the tier counters say the foreground never waited on any of it.

Spec budget check, the read tier table in 08-performance.md: the cache tier plays the LFC row, budget 50 to 100 us.
gamingpc measures p50 8 us and p99 16 us, server3 p50 64 us with a p99 of 16 ms that is the price of sharing 8 cores with your own pgbench.
The store tier row only gets tested by a run that misses, and this workload's point is that it does not.

## cold-attach, process spawn to first row

| label | p50 ms | p95 ms | max ms | connect p50 | store ops per cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zou-localfs-gamingpc | 20.5 | 21.9 | 49.7 | 19.9 | 3 manifest gets, 13 small, 65 probes |
| zou-minio-server3 | 245.7 | 501.1 | 1329.1 | 239.8 | same shape |

Each cycle spawns the postmaster, polls the socket every millisecond, runs `select abalance from pgbench_accounts where aid = 1`, and stops the server.
The budget is 300 ms to first row.
gamingpc measures 20.5 ms at the median with the worst of 20 cycles at 49.7 ms.
server3 measures 245.7 ms at the median, 13 of 20 cycles under the budget, with a 1.3 s worst cycle.

Neither box read a single page or checkpoint object from the store during attach: the page cache warmed by the select leg survives restart and answered all 1860 reads per box, cache tier, in both runs.
What separates 20 ms from 245 ms is coordination round trips, not data: the same 3 manifest gets and 65 zero byte range probes per cycle cost microseconds against a local filesystem and single digit milliseconds each through the docker proxy to MinIO, and they are paid mostly in series during boot.
That makes the probe fan the obvious next target if attach ever needs to be faster on an object store: the data path is already not the problem.

## Reading

The three exit claims measure as follows.
Cold attach under 300 ms: met at the median on both boxes, 20.5 ms warm local and 245.7 ms against MinIO, with the honest caveat that the MinIO tail crosses the line, p95 501 ms.
Read tiers inside budgets: the cache tier answers at 8 to 64 us median against a 50 to 100 us budget, and no other tier was exercised because nothing missed.
The MinIO select rerun served from cache: 12 gets and 8 KB against v1's 3.4 GB, 90x the throughput, case closed.

The tier counters land in the same counter file the op counters live in, so one `--zoustats` flag captures both, and any run of any scenario now reports where its reads were served from.
