# 2026-08-12: eight hundred mostly idle projects, and the bill they write while asleep

M1b borrows a scenario from Neon's own pricing page, eight hundred mostly idle tenants, and asks for the whole fleet under 90 dollars a month.
This is the first run that measures it instead of modelling it, and the answer is 294.18 a month on S3 standard, a bit over three times the line.
Almost none of that is data.
The bytes for all eight hundred come to 76 cents and the other 293.42 is requests, every one of them written by a project nobody is talking to.

One box.
gamingpc, i9-13900K, 32 threads, 32 GB, Ubuntu 26.04 under WSL2, store on local disk, node pinned to cpus 0-7 with taskset so the eight cores are the deployment and the harness is not sharing them.
The store is a local directory, so the dollars are the S3 standard and R2 price cards dated 2026-08-01 applied to the operations this run counted, not a bill anyone paid.
Nothing is simulated, and the fleet was built from empty: 800 tenants made, 0 skipped.

`fleet-800-idle` is 800 tenants, 100 attached at once, `shared_buffers` 16MB each, 16 clients, 600 s warmup, a 300 s window capped at 200 rps, 600 s idle, a 300 s churn window drawing from all eight hundred, then 90 minutes of hold with the first 180 s discarded and a sample every 30 s.
The hold window is the new part and it is the reason for the run.
It watches a fleet nobody is using and counts what that fleet does to the object store anyway.

## Provisioning, this time from empty

| tenants | made | skipped | jobs | seconds | tenants a minute | cold create p50 | p99 | first request p50 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 800 | 800 | 0 | 8 | 9346.5 | 5.14 | 10.502 ms | 47.038 ms | 92.08 s | 107.97 s |

The M1b line is a hundred tenants a second sustained and this is 0.086 a second, a bit over a thousand times short.
It is also the first honest reading of that line, because the thousand tenant page reported 36.2 a minute off a run that made 63 tenants and skipped the 937 earlier passes had already built.
Registering the ref is still a small write, 10.5 ms at the median.
Everything after it is initdb, the genesis capture and the first attach, eight at a time on eight cores for two and a half hours, which is where first request at 92 s comes from: a tenant's first row waits behind the seven others in its batch and behind the queue that formed while they ran.

## Steady, and churn over eight hundred

| phase | tenants drawn from | touched | requests | errors | rps | p50 ms | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| steady, working set attached | 100 | 100 | 59999 | 0 | 200 | 0.692 | 0.928 | 1.236 | 6.244 |
| churn, all eight hundred | 800 | 735 | 2091 | 3 | 6.97 | 1852.7 | 3363.0 | 11941.1 | 14161.3 |

Steady says the same thing the thousand tenant node said.
Once a project is up, a node holding hundreds of them serves reads in well under a millisecond and a half at the tail, sixty thousand requests with none dropped, and it is flat across request types: row by pk p99 1.210 ms, page of 20 p99 1.251 ms, filtered page p99 1.255 ms.
The registry served 350106 hits against 1684 misses.

Churn is attach again, and it is cheaper than it was.
The node turned over 4962 attaches in 300 s at a 2.263 s mean while holding the gauge at 100, against 1758 at a 7.275 s mean on the thousand tenant run the day before.
The two runs are not a controlled comparison, the fleet is smaller and the node is a day of commits newer, so what is on the page is the pair of numbers rather than a cause.
Three requests failed out of 2091, two of them the harness timeout and one a 503 from a project whose connection closed under it.

## The hold window, which is the point

Ninety minutes, no requests, 175 samples.
Across the eight hundred, a project spent 540 s of that window attached and 4680 s dormant, and the two states could not be further apart.

| state | puts an hour | gets an hour | lists | deletes an hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dormant | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| attached | 742.9 | 762.9 | 0 | 55.6 |

A dormant project is free.
Not cheap, free: zero operations an hour, and the only thing it costs is the bytes it left behind.
That is scale to zero working exactly as the design says it should, and it is worth writing down because it is the half of the result that is good.

An attached project with nobody asking it for anything puts an object every 4.85 seconds and reads one every 4.72.
That timer is the WAL lease heartbeat.
The lease TTL is 15 s (`crates/zou-pg/src/lib.rs`, `WAL_LEASE_TTL_SECS`) and the heartbeat renews at a third of it with jitter, and a renewal is a manifest read plus a conditional write, so 720 gets and 720 puts an hour per attached project before anything else happens.
Measured is 762.9 and 742.9, so the heartbeat is 94 to 97 percent of it and the rest is other periodic work.
The hold window counts operations without their key class, so that attribution is arithmetic against the timer rather than a class breakdown, and the steady window agrees in shape: 18251 of 21474 conditional writes there are manifest writes.

## What eight hundred cost

| card | storage | requests | total a month | per project |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aws-s3-standard | 0.76 | 293.42 | 294.18 | 0.3677 |
| cloudflare-r2 | 0.50 | 264.07 | 264.57 | 0.3307 |

Extrapolated to a month at the hold window's rates that is 54.2 million puts, 55.7 million gets, 4.1 million deletes and no lists.
Compute is not priced on either row, so these are storage bills and nothing else.

Two readings follow, and they point in opposite directions.
Per project the number is good: 37 cents a month all in on S3 standard against the roughly 1.10 Neon models for the same scenario, and 33 cents on R2.
The fleet number misses because the scenario holds an eighth of the fleet attached the whole time, and 294 of the 294 dollars are the hundred attached projects renewing leases twelve hundred times a minute between them with nobody watching.

Changing the price card does not fix this, R2 is still 264.
Two things would.
An attached project with no traffic could stop renewing and let the lease lapse, taking the same restore an eviction would cost if somebody came back, or the renewal interval could scale with idleness rather than sitting at a third of a fifteen second TTL forever.
That is a zou change and not a benchmark change, and it is filed there.

## Reading

The scale to zero claim holds and the eight hundred tenant cost line does not, and both come out of the same table.
A project nobody uses costs zero operations an hour if it is dormant and 1561 an hour if it is attached, and the whole M1b miss lives in that difference.
The fix is a quieter idle attached project, not a cheaper store.
