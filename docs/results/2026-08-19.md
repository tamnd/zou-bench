# 2026-08-19: the same hundred thousand sockets on a quiet box, and the rate they were supposed to have

Two days ago server3 held a hundred thousand sockets and delivered to all of them, and the rows a second were 62.8 of a thousand.
The write up that day said the write path was what stood in the way and named the engine's commit path as the thing to look at.
This is the day that was checked, and the answer is that the commit path was not it.

zou gained a seven step decomposition of the commit path in tamnd/zou#491 and this repository learned to read it in #32, so both halves of this day are measured rather than argued.

## The box, said up front

gamingpc, a 13th generation Core i9-13900K with 32 cores and 47 GB, Ubuntu inside WSL2, nothing else running on it.
That is the whole reason it is here: server3 shares eight cores with the owner's crawler and a MinIO on a disk that is 95 percent full, and the question of the day was whether that mattered.

One thing about these runs is not up to standard and it is said here rather than in a footnote.
The generator was on the same box as the node, which the socket runs in this repository are not allowed to do, because a hundred thousand descriptors and a goroutine each on the node under test makes the node's cpu partly the benchmark.
gamingpc's Ubuntu is inside WSL2 behind Windows NAT, so nothing off the machine reaches the node without a port proxy nobody has set up yet, and the boxes that could have generated from outside are the busy ones this day was trying to get away from.
So the delivery percentiles below are not comparable to the server3 ones and the socket count is not being asserted by them.
What they can carry is the node's own commit, because a generator stealing cpu makes that worse rather than better, and it did not get worse.

The sockets went out over six loopback source addresses, since one address has one ephemeral port range and 28,231 ports is not a hundred thousand sockets to the same destination.
The node was `zou dev` with a directory store, every realtime quota turned off, and a descriptor limit of 200000.

## The write path on its own, first

Before any sockets, five runs of pgbench where every transaction inserts a batch of rows, on a fresh store each time, with the commit steps read out of the counter file afterwards.

| store | writers, rows a transaction | window | tps | rows a second | pgbench latency mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| directory | 8, 25 | 60 s | 1307 | 32,683 | 6.1 ms |
| directory | 16, 250 | 60 s | 1173 | 293,214 | 13.6 ms |
| MinIO on loopback | 8, 25 | 60 s | 1152 | 28,790 | 6.9 ms |
| MinIO on loopback | 16, 250 | 60 s | 997 | 249,271 | 16.0 ms |
| MinIO on loopback | 8, 25 | 300 s | 937 | 23,419 | 8.5 ms |

Eight writers at twenty five rows a transaction against a MinIO is the shape that gave 62.8 rows a second on server3.
Here it gives 28,790, and the five minute version of it holds 23,419 with a worst single commit of 131 ms.

The commit steps from that five minute run, in microseconds, as power of two bucket upper bounds.

| step | samples | p50 | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| push | 87674 | 4096 | 8192 | 16384 | 4194304 |
| stage | 87675 | 32 | 64 | 64 | 512 |
| window | 47922 | 4096 | 4096 | 4096 | 8192 |
| dispatch | 47922 | 32 | 64 | 64 | 4096 |
| put | 47922 | 4096 | 16384 | 32768 | 131072 |
| ack | 47922 | 2 | 2 | 16 | 32768 |
| durable | 87675 | 8192 | 16384 | 32768 | 131072 |

Three of the seven are microseconds.
stage, dispatch and ack together are under 200 us at the p99, so the sequencer, the pipelined landing and the acks that resolve in strict chain order cost nothing worth naming, and the group commit is doing its job: 47922 batches carried 87675 chunks and 281040 transactions.
What a commit waits on is the three millisecond batch window, the store put at four milliseconds, and the pusher's own cadence, and durable at eight milliseconds is close to the sum of them.
push's maximum is the same 4.19 s in every leg including the sixty second ones, which is the gap the probe leaves between loading the table and starting pgbench rather than a stall.

A directory store and a MinIO are within fifteen percent of each other on throughput and within a millisecond on the commit mean, so at these rates the store is not what sets the floor.

Every rung asked for its full rate and got it, and every delivery owed arrived.
The rate is the thing that was missing on server3 and it is not missing here.

The commit steps under the hundred thousand, same units:

| step | samples | p50 | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| push | 4613 | 4096 | 262144 | 262144 | 67108864 |
| stage | 4614 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 2048 |
| window | 2435 | 4096 | 4096 | 8192 | 16384 |
| dispatch | 2435 | 64 | 2048 | 4096 | 8192 |
| put | 2435 | 2048 | 8192 | 16384 | 32768 |
| ack | 2435 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 8192 |
| durable | 4614 | 8192 | 16384 | 16384 | 32768 |

durable is 8 ms at the median and 16 ms at the p99 with a hundred thousand sockets attached and thirty million deliveries going out under them, which is the same 8 ms the write probe measured with nothing attached at all.
dispatch is the only step that moved, from 64 us to 2 ms at the p95, which is the inflight bound doing what it exists for while the box is busy.
push reads as 262 ms at the p95 and that is not a stall: the writers are rate limited to a thousand rows a second, so the pusher is asleep for most of its loop and the sleep is inside that step by construction, which tamnd/zou#492 now says where the number is read.

The node is the same binary doing the same work, so what is different is the box, and specifically eight cores shared with a crawler and a MinIO on the same disk as the WAL the pusher reads and the pages the checkpointer writes.

## What this does not settle

The delivery percentiles here are not a published number, because the generator was on the node.
tamnd/zou#493 is the one run that finishes the M4 line, the same `sockets-100k` scenario with the node quiet and the generator somewhere else, and it names the two ways to get the boxes.
Until then the socket count stands on server3's run and the rate stands on this one, and neither of them is one run with both.
